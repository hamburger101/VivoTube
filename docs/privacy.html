Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 11, 2026

This policy explains what information VivoTube, an unofficial YouTube client for the PlayStation Vita, accesses, stores and shares. It covers the VivoTube app and this website. VivoTube is not made by, endorsed by, or connected to YouTube or Google.

In short:

  • VivoTube has no servers of its own. Its developer never receives your personal information.
  • Everything VivoTube saves stays on your Vita's memory card.
  • VivoTube connects only to Google and YouTube (to show and play videos, and for your account if you sign in) and to GitHub (to check for updates).
  • There are no ads, analytics, tracking or cookies.

VivoTube uses YouTube API Services

VivoTube uses the YouTube API Services, and it uses YouTube's own services to search, browse and play videos. Information that reaches Google or YouTube is handled under the Google Privacy Policy. By using VivoTube you also agree to the YouTube Terms of Service; see VivoTube's Terms of Service.

If you sign in with Google

Signing in is optional. If you sign in, you give VivoTube permission, through Google, to manage your YouTube account. VivoTube uses that permission only for these things:

  • Reads your channel's name, handle and profile picture, to show who is signed in.
  • Reads your subscriptions, your playlists and the videos in them, and your liked videos, to show them in the app and keep the app's library in sync.
  • Reads your rating (like or dislike) of a video when you open it.
  • Changes your account only when you choose to: subscribing to or unsubscribing from a channel, liking, disliking or removing a rating from a video, and adding a video to one of your playlists.

Sign-in uses an OAuth client that VivoTube's developer registered with Google, unless you set up your own. Your sign-in goes directly between your Vita and Google; it never passes through the developer. Google shows the developer only totals, such as how many accounts have signed in and how many requests VivoTube made, not who you are or what you did.

VivoTube doesn't upload videos, post comments, read your email address, or use your Google account for anything beyond YouTube. Google account information goes only between your Vita and Google. It isn't sold, isn't used for advertising, and isn't transferred to anyone else. VivoTube's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

What VivoTube stores on your Vita

VivoTube saves its files in ux0:data/VivoTube/ on your memory card. They're ordinary files: anyone who can open your memory card can read them. Nothing in this folder is uploaded anywhere.

  • Your sign-in (account.json): the token Google issued to VivoTube, plus your channel's name, handle and picture address.
  • Your library: watch history and where you stopped in each video, search history, subscriptions, likes and dislikes, Watch later, playlists, and hidden recommendations.
  • Your settings, such as language, location, captions and playback options.
  • Your own Google OAuth client (google_client.json), only if you set one up instead of the built-in one.
  • A log (log.txt) of what the app did during the last session, used to diagnose problems. It can include video IDs and error messages. It stays on your Vita unless you choose to share it, for example in a bug report.
  • An update package, only if an update downloads but can't be installed, so you can install it by hand.

Who VivoTube talks to

Google and YouTube

To work, VivoTube sends requests from your Vita to YouTube and Google: the searches you type, the videos, channels and pages you open, your subscriptions' upload feeds, and requests for pictures and video streams. If you're signed in, requests about your account carry your sign-in. Like any website you visit, Google and YouTube see your IP address. YouTube also gives VivoTube a temporary visitor identifier, which VivoTube keeps in memory only and forgets when it closes. The Google Privacy Policy describes how Google handles this.

GitHub

When it starts, VivoTube asks GitHub for the latest VivoTube release, and it downloads the release from GitHub if you choose to update. GitHub sees your IP address, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

No one else

VivoTube has no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking of any kind, and it doesn't let any other party show content or ads in the app. VivoTube shows videos, pictures and text from YouTube. It doesn't use cookies, and it doesn't read anything on your Vita other than its own files.

This website

This website is hosted by GitHub Pages. It has no cookies, analytics or third-party scripts. GitHub may keep server logs, including your IP address, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Removing your information

  • Sign out in VivoTube (Sign out in the side menu, or your account picture at the top right). VivoTube asks Google to cancel its access and deletes the saved sign-in from your Vita.
  • Revoke access from Google at any time, even without your Vita: open the Google security settings page and remove VivoTube's access.
  • Clear history in VivoTube's Settings, under History & privacy.
  • Delete everything by deleting the ux0:data/VivoTube folder, for example with VitaShell.

Signing out or deleting files on your Vita doesn't change anything on your YouTube account, such as your subscriptions, likes or YouTube's own watch history. Manage those on YouTube.

Children

VivoTube isn't directed at children. Using YouTube is subject to the age requirements in the YouTube Terms of Service.

Changes to this policy

If VivoTube starts handling information differently, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change. Earlier versions are in the project's history.

Contact

For questions or complaints about VivoTube's privacy practices, open an issue on GitHub.
